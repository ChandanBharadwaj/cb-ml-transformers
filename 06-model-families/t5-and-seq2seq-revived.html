T5 & Seq2Seq Revived

Short page, deliberately — the third family needs context more than depth. T5 (2019) resurrected M4.3's encoder-decoder shape with transformer guts and one radical framing: every NLP task is text-to-text. It also ran the field's most systematic bake-off of pretraining choices — receipts worth citing in interviews — and its descendants still quietly run translation and speech systems you use.

Everything is text-to-text

Span corruption, and the great bake-off

T5's pretraining objective sits between BERT's and GPT's: mask out random spans (not single tokens) with sentinel markers, and train the decoder to regenerate just the missing spans — "Thank you <X> me to your party <Y> week" → "<X> for inviting <Y> last". The paper's larger contribution was methodological: it systematically compared objectives (LM vs MLM vs span corruption), architectures, data sizes and fine-tuning schemes under one budget. Two conclusions to carry: denoising objectives beat plain LM for downstream fine-tuning at that scale, and most clever variations mattered less than scale and data quality — an early rhyme of M7.4's scaling laws.

Where enc-dec survives, and why decoders still won

Encoder-decoder remains the right tool where the input deserves full bidirectional reading and the output is a faithful transform: translation (production systems), Whisper (audio encoder → text decoder), BART-style summarization, and — a fact worth knowing — Flan-T5 remained the best instruction-following model per parameter for years. But the general race went decoder-only (M5.8's four reasons: universality, objective simplicity, in-context learning, serving economics). T5's text-to-text vision won culturally even as its architecture lost the scale race: "everything is a prompt, every answer is text" is the modern interface.

Real numbers

T5 sizes: Small 60M → Base 220M (≈ BERT-base + GPT-2-small stapled together) → XXL 11B. Trained on C4 — 750GB of cleaned CommonCrawl the paper open-sourced, which became the seed of many later corpora (FineWeb, from M6.1's build, is a descendant). Whisper, the enc-dec you've most likely used this week: 1.5B params, 680k hours of audio — cross-attention (M5.8) letting text tokens attend over audio frames.

Connect the dots

T5's fingerprints on your daily tools: the ▁-prefixed tokens you may have seen in HF tokenizer dumps are its SentencePiece legacy (M5.1); "instruction: … output: …" dataset formats descend from its task prefixes; and if your product ever transcribes audio, cross-attention from this family is doing the work. Course position check: you now hold all three silhouettes with their native objectives — MLM/encoder, causal/decoder, span-corruption/enc-dec. The families are complete; next page is the field guide for choosing among their descendants.

Build: drive T5's prefixes yourself
from transformers import pipeline

t5 = pipeline("text2text-generation", model="google/flan-t5-base")

for prompt in [
    "Translate English to German: The house is wonderful.",
    "Summarize: The transformer architecture, introduced in 2017, replaced "
    "recurrent networks by using attention to process all tokens in parallel, "
    "enabling much larger models and faster training on modern hardware.",
    "Is this sentence grammatical? 'The car drove quickly street.'",
    "Answer: What is the capital of Karnataka?",
]:
    print("\n>", prompt[:60], "...")
    print(" ", t5(prompt, max_new_tokens=40)[0]["generated_text"])

Flan-T5-base: 220M parameters — 1/800th of GPT-3 — following instructions credibly on CPU. Worth experiencing to calibrate "how much model does my task actually need?", a question M8.6 turns into a decision framework.

If you remember one thing

T5 = the M5.8 encoder-decoder + span-corruption pretraining + one big idea: every task is text-to-text. The architecture now specializes (translation, Whisper); the idea became the industry's interface. And its bake-off left the receipts: objectives matter some, scale and data matter more.

Interview questions

What is span corruption and how does it differ from BERT's MLM?

Contiguous spans (mean length ~3) are replaced by single sentinel tokens, and the decoder generates the concatenated missing spans autoregressively. Differences from MLM: targets are generated as a sequence (training the decoder properly), spans are harder than isolated tokens (more context integration), and the output is shorter than the input (efficient). It's a denoising objective adapted to the enc-dec shape — same family as BART's corruptions.

What did the T5 paper's systematic study conclude, roughly?

Under controlled budgets: denoising/span objectives beat plain LM for fine-tuned downstream quality; encoder-decoder with shared vocab was strong for text-to-text; most architectural/objective tweaks yielded marginal gains compared to scaling model, data and training — foreshadowing scaling laws. Its enduring artifacts: the C4 corpus and the text-to-text framing. Citing "T5's ablations showed recipe < scale" is a compact, credible interview move.

Why does Whisper use an encoder-decoder rather than decoder-only?

The input (audio) is a complete, fixed signal deserving full bidirectional encoding — there's no causality constraint on listening. The decoder then generates text attending over those audio representations via cross-attention. Decoder-only would force audio through the same causal stream as text, wasting the ability to hear the whole utterance before writing. General rule: when input and output are different modalities or the input is fully available upfront, enc-dec is natural.

Did T5's "text-to-text" idea win or lose?

Both, instructively: the architecture lost the general-purpose race to decoder-only (M5.8's economics), but the interface won completely — modern LLM usage ("instruction in, text out", classification answered as words, numbers as digits) is T5's framing executed by a different architecture. Flan-T5 also proved instruction tuning's power per parameter before it was standard. Good answer-shape for interviews: separate the fate of the idea from the fate of the implementation.
