Decoder, Masking & Generation

The encoder you just built reads bidirectionally — "bank" saw "river" on its right. Perfect for understanding; disqualifying for generation. A model trained to predict the next word (M4.1) must never peek at it. One triangular mask converts your encoder into a decoder — the GPT architecture — and explains, mechanically, why your chatbot streams tokens and why output tokens cost more than input tokens. This page is the second black box's floor plan.

The causal mask: a staircase of −∞

The change is embarrassingly small. In self-attention's score grid (5.3's QKᵀ), before the softmax, set every score where a token would look at a later position to −∞. Softmax turns −∞ into exactly 0 weight (e^−∞ = 0) — the future contributes nothing to the blend. Everything else — Q/K/V, heads, residuals, FFN, stacking — is untouched.

Training: predict everywhere, in parallel

Here's the elegance that makes decoder pretraining so efficient. With the mask in place, every position can be trained simultaneously: position 1 predicts token 2, position 2 predicts token 3, … all in one forward pass, because the mask guarantees no position saw its own answer. A 1,000-token document yields 1,000 next-token exercises for the price of one pass — cross-entropy (M3.2) at every position, averaged, backprop, step. This is how "trained on 15 trillion tokens" is even feasible: the mask turns the whole corpus into dense, parallel supervision (and this is where 3.3's "LLM pretraining is less than one epoch" gets its denominator).

Inference: the loop that explains your streaming UI

One honest asterisk for completeness: training feeds each position the true previous tokens (teacher forcing), while generation feeds the model its own previous outputs — so early mistakes can compound in ways training never showed it (exposure bias). It's a known, mostly-tolerated mismatch; RLHF-era tuning (M8.4) partially addresses it by training on the model's own generations.

Real numbers

The two-phase cost structure, in your API bill: prefill processes the whole prompt in parallel (compute-heavy, cheap per token), then decode emits ~20–100 tokens/second serially. That's why time-to-first-token and tokens-per-second are quoted separately in every serving benchmark — and why input tokens cost roughly 3–5× less than output tokens on most APIs. A 2,000-token RAG prompt + 300-token answer spends most of its wall-clock on those 300 serial steps.

Connect the dots

Mysteries from your production life, now mechanical: streaming UIs exist because tokens are physically produced one at a time — the stream is the computation, not a UX flourish. Output pricing reflects serial full passes per token. max_tokens caps the loop's iterations. And "the model started strong then went off the rails" is exposure bias plus sampling compounding — each slightly-off token becomes conditioning for the next. Your second black box now has moving parts you can name.

Build: implement the mask and the loop
import numpy as np

def softmax_rows(Z):
    e = np.exp(Z - Z.max(-1, keepdims=True)); return e / e.sum(-1, keepdims=True)

# --- the causal mask, from scratch ---
n = 4
S = np.random.default_rng(0).normal(size=(n, n))     # pretend scores (QK^T/√d)
mask = np.triu(np.ones((n, n)), k=1).astype(bool)    # upper triangle = future
S[mask] = -1e9                                       # the page's −∞
A = softmax_rows(S)
print(A.round(3))          # upper triangle is 0.000 — future erased ✓

# --- the generation loop, with a real model ---
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM
import torch
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("gpt2")
lm  = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("gpt2")
ids = tok("The capital of France", return_tensors="pt").input_ids
for _ in range(8):                                   # the animation, literally
    logits = lm(ids).logits[0, -1]
    nxt = logits.argmax()                            # greedy (T=0)
    ids = torch.cat([ids, nxt.view(1, 1)], dim=1)
    print(tok.decode(ids[0]))

Watch the sentence grow one token per iteration — your chatbot's streaming, reproduced in nine lines. Then swap argmax for temperature sampling (torch.multinomial(torch.softmax(logits/0.8, -1), 1)) and run twice: different outputs, same model — M11.1's whole topic, previewed.

If you remember one thing

Decoder = encoder + a triangular mask (future scores → −∞ → weight 0). Training: all positions predict their next token in one parallel pass. Inference: generate–append–repeat, one full pass per output token — which is why LLMs stream, why output costs more, and why the KV cache had to be invented. Interview questions

What is causal (masked) self-attention and why is it needed?

Self-attention where each position may only attend to itself and earlier positions, enforced by setting future-position scores to −∞ before softmax (→ exactly zero weight). Needed because a next-token predictor trained with visibility of the future would learn to copy the answer rather than model language — the mask makes the training task honest while keeping training fully parallel across positions.

Why is decoder TRAINING parallel but INFERENCE serial?

Training: the full target text exists, so with the causal mask every position predicts its next token simultaneously in one pass — n training examples per sequence per step. Inference: token t+1's distribution depends on token t, which must be generated first — the dependency chain is real, so generation is inherently one-token-per-pass. This asymmetry drives all serving economics: cheap parallel prefill, expensive serial decode.

Architecturally, what's the difference between BERT and GPT?

Nearly nothing but the mask and the objective: BERT = encoder blocks (bidirectional attention) trained with masked-LM (M6.2); GPT = the same blocks with causal masking, trained on next-token prediction. The tiny difference dictates use: bidirectional context → understanding/embedding tasks; causal → generation. (Plus detail differences: pre/post-LN, positions, tokenizers — but the mask is the essence.)

What are prefill and decode phases, and why do they perform so differently?

Prefill: the prompt's tokens processed in one parallel pass — big matrix multiplies, compute-bound, high throughput. Decode: each new token requires a full serial pass conditioned on everything so far — small matrices per step, memory-bandwidth-bound, ~20–100 tok/s. Consequences: time-to-first-token ≈ prefill, generation speed ≈ decode, output tokens priced above input, and the KV cache (M11.2) exists to stop decode from recomputing the past.

What is exposure bias?

The train/inference mismatch of autoregressive models: training always conditions on ground-truth prefixes (teacher forcing), but at inference the model conditions on its own possibly-imperfect outputs — a distribution it never saw during pretraining, so early errors can compound. Mitigations in practice: better sampling (nucleus), RLHF-style tuning on model-generated text, and self-correction patterns. Worth naming precisely; interviewers often accept the concept without the term, but the term marks depth.
