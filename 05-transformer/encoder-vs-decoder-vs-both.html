Encoder vs Decoder vs Both

Module capstone. You've built the encoder block and the causal mask — every transformer in existence is one of three arrangements of what you now know. This page is the field guide: the three silhouettes, the one mechanism you haven't met yet (cross-attention), how to pick by task — and why the LLM era quietly standardized on just one of the three.

The three silhouettes

Choosing by task: the decision table

Your stack, sorted in one breath: all-MiniLM = encoder (you want vectors, not text). GLiNER = encoder (labels spans, generates nothing). Your chatbot's LLM = decoder. A translation service = enc-dec. The sorting rule is one question: is the output a vector, a label, or new text?

Why decoder-only won the LLM era

In 2019 all three families looked viable; by 2023, frontier models were uniformly decoder-only. Four compounding reasons: (1) Task universality — with everything framed as text-continuation, one architecture handles translation, QA, code, and chat as prompts, no per-task heads. (2) Training simplicity — one objective (next token) on raw text; no masking-rate choices, no input/output pairing needed. (3) In-context learning — the emergent few-shot behavior (M6.3) showed up most strongly in decoders, replacing fine-tuning with prompting for many tasks. (4) Serving economics — the KV cache (M11.2) makes appending-to-a-conversation cheap for decoders, exactly the access pattern of chat. Encoder-decoder survives where inputs deserve first-class bidirectional treatment (Whisper, translation); encoders survive wherever vectors are the product — i.e., your embedding stack.

Real numbers

The family tree's split, by the numbers you now read fluently: BERT-base (2018, encoder): d=768, L=12 → 110M. GPT-2 (2019, decoder): d=768, L=12 → 117M — nearly identical bodies, one mask apart. T5-base (2019, enc-dec): 220M ≈ both bodies stapled together. Then the fork: encoders stopped scaling (BERT-large, 340M, remains the family's practical ceiling), while decoders ran to 175B (GPT-3) and beyond — the "one task, infinite data" objective is what kept paying at scale.

Connect the dots

The map page's promise is now redeemable: both black boxes are the same machine. MiniLM = 5.6's block × 6, unmasked, width 384. Your chat LLM = the same block × ~30–100, masked, width 4096+. One reads and emits geometry; one writes and emits tokens. Everything from here on — pretraining objectives (M6), compute (M7), fine-tuning (M8), MiniLM's training (M9), GLiNER (M10) — is about how these three silhouettes get filled with knowledge, not about new machinery.

from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModel
from bertviz import head_view

name = "bert-base-uncased"
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(name)
model = AutoModel.from_pretrained(name, output_attentions=True)

sentence = "The cat chased the mice because it was hungry"
inputs = tok(sentence, return_tensors="pt")
attn = model(**inputs).attentions          # 12 layers × 12 heads of REAL weights

head_view(attn, tok.convert_ids_to_tokens(inputs.input_ids[0]))
# (opens an interactive view — run in Jupyter/Colab)

If you remember one thing

Three arrangements of one block: encoder (bidirectional → vectors: BERT/MiniLM/GLiNER), decoder (masked → generation: every chat LLM), encoder-decoder (both + cross-attention: translation/Whisper). Cross-attention = Q from the writer, K/V from the reader — 2015's attention with its formal name. Decoder-only won the LLM era on universality, training simplicity, in-context learning and serving economics. The transformer is now, fully, yours.

Interview questions

Encoder-only vs decoder-only vs encoder-decoder — when do you use each?

Encoder-only (BERT/MiniLM): bidirectional attention, outputs contextual vectors — embeddings, classification, NER, retrieval. Decoder-only (GPT/Llama): causal attention, outputs next-token distributions — all open-ended generation and chat. Encoder-decoder (T5/Whisper/translation): encoder digests the input bidirectionally, decoder generates while cross-attending to it — best when output must faithfully transform a fully-understood input. Decision heuristic: is the product a vector, or new text, or text-conditioned-on-fully-read-input?

What exactly is cross-attention and where does it appear?

Attention where queries come from one sequence (the decoder's states) and keys/values from another (the encoder's outputs): softmax(Q_dec · K_encᵀ/√d) · V_enc. It sits as a third sublayer in each encoder-decoder decoder block, between self-attention and the FFN. It's exactly Bahdanau-2015 attention formalized — and the same pattern reappears in multimodal models (text queries attending over image-encoder outputs).

Why did decoder-only become the standard for large models?

(1) Universality: every task expressible as text-continuation — one model, no task heads. (2) The simplest, densest objective: next-token prediction over raw text, no masking-rate or pairing decisions. (3) In-context learning emerged strongest in this family, letting prompting replace fine-tuning. (4) Serving: causal structure + KV caching matches chat's append-only access pattern perfectly. Encoders didn't die — they specialized into the embedding/retrieval layer (your MiniLM), which is arguably the second-most-deployed transformer type on earth.

Could you use a decoder-only LLM to produce embeddings? Trade-offs?

Yes — take a hidden state (e.g., last token's, or mean-pooled) — and recent LLM-based embedders do exactly this competitively at large scale. Trade-offs: causal attention means early tokens never see later ones (mitigated by tricks like repeating the text or bidirectional-finetuning); cost is much higher than a 22M encoder; and off-the-shelf decoder states aren't trained for similarity geometry (need contrastive fine-tuning, M9.3). For most retrieval workloads, dedicated small encoders still win on cost-performance — which is why MiniLM survives in your stack next to a frontier LLM.

BERT can see both directions — why can't it generate text well?

Generation requires the autoregressive factorization P(next | prefix); BERT was trained to fill isolated masked slots given both sides — a denoising objective, not a sequential one. It has no principled way to produce token t+1 conditioned only on a growing prefix, and iterative mask-filling generation is slow and incoherent in practice. Objective determines capability: bidirectional training buys understanding at the price of generation (and vice versa for GPT — which is why both families exist).
