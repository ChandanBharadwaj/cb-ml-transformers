Multi-Head Attention

One attention pass produces one glance pattern per token — one softmax row, one blend. But language demands several kinds of looking at once: a verb needs its subject (syntax), a pronoun needs its referent (coreference), everything cares a bit about its neighbors (position). One blend can't serve all masters. The fix is almost comically direct: run several smaller attentions in parallel and let each specialize.

Split the space, not the bill

Multi-head attention doesn't multiply the cost — it divides the dimensions. MiniLM's 384-dim space splits into 12 heads × 32 dims each: every head gets its own small W_Q, W_K, W_V, computes last page's recipe entirely inside its 32-dim slice, and produces its own attention pattern and its own blended output. Same total parameters and FLOPs as one full-width head — but twelve independent glance patterns instead of one.

Why subspaces beat one big head

The deep reason this works: a single attention pattern is a single softmax over positions — it can't simultaneously put most weight on "the subject" and "the previous token" when they're different positions; the weights compete inside one distribution. Twelve independent softmaxes remove the competition. And each head's small 32-dim slice acts as a learned filter: a projection that keeps only the features its specialty needs (case/number for the syntax head; gender/animacy for coreference) and ignores the rest — dot products in a subspace measure agreement only on the retained features (M1.3's "matrices as filters", cashing in).

MultiHead(X) = concat(head₁ … head_h) · W_O,  head_i = Attn(XW_Q⁽ⁱ⁾, XW_K⁽ⁱ⁾, XW_V⁽ⁱ⁾)
In plain words: h copies of last page's recipe, each in a d/h-dim slice with its own three matrices; glue the h outputs side by side; one final learned mix (W_O) lets the heads' findings talk to each other. Four matrices total per layer: W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O.
Real numbers

Head arithmetic across the family you know: MiniLM-L6: 12 heads × 32 dims (384 total), 6 layers = 72 heads all told. BERT-base: 12 × 64 (768), 12 layers = 144 heads. GPT-3: 96 × 128 (12,288), 96 layers = 9,216 heads. Pruning studies' famous result: after training, 30–50% of heads can often be removed with minimal loss — specialization is uneven, redundancy is real, and that redundancy is partly why distillation to a small student (M9.4, the MiniLM story) works at all.

Connect the dots

When M9.4 explains how MiniLM was distilled from a big teacher, the exact objects being imitated are these heads' attention patterns — the student learns to glance like the teacher. And when you run the M5.8 capstone (BERTviz) you'll scroll through these very specialists in a real model — find the previous-token head first; it's unmissable. One more connection: "GQA" in modern LLM specs (M11.2) is a memory optimization that makes several query-heads share one key/value head — you now have every concept that sentence needs.

Build: one attention head in 25 lines — verify everything
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

def softmax_rows(Z):
    e = np.exp(Z - Z.max(axis=-1, keepdims=True))
    return e / e.sum(axis=-1, keepdims=True)

n, d_model, d_head = 4, 8, 4                 # 4 tokens, tiny dims
X   = rng.normal(size=(n, d_model))          # token vectors (post-embedding)
W_Q = rng.normal(size=(d_model, d_head)) / np.sqrt(d_model)
W_K = rng.normal(size=(d_model, d_head)) / np.sqrt(d_model)
W_V = rng.normal(size=(d_model, d_head)) / np.sqrt(d_model)

Q, K, V = X @ W_Q, X @ W_K, X @ W_V          # three projections   (n × d_head)
S = Q @ K.T / np.sqrt(d_head)                # all-pairs scores    (n × n)
A = softmax_rows(S)                          # attention weights   (n × n)
out = A @ V                                  # blended values      (n × d_head)

print("attention matrix:\n", A.round(3))
print("rows sum to 1? ", np.allclose(A.sum(axis=1), 1.0))   # must be True
print("output shape:", out.shape)

# multi-head = this, h times, then concat and mix:
# heads = [head(X) for _ in range(h)]; out = np.concatenate(heads, -1) @ W_O

Checks to run: rows sum to 1 (softmax sanity); make two tokens' X rows identical and watch them get identical attention rows (content-addressing, live); scale S by 100 and watch A become one-hot (the √d lesson again). Twenty-five lines, and you have implemented the machine this course is named after.

If you remember one thing

Multi-head = the same attention recipe, run h times in parallel on learned subspace slices, then concatenated and mixed. Cost of one big head, but h independent glance patterns — syntax, coreference, position — because one softmax can't serve several masters. Heads specialize by training, not by design, and their redundancy is real (and exploitable).

Interview questions

Why multiple heads instead of one attention with the full dimension?

One head = one softmax distribution per token — a single glance pattern, forced to compromise between different relationship types (syntactic, referential, positional) that want weight on different positions. h heads give h independent distributions in h learned subspaces, letting patterns coexist without competing. Since dimensions are split (d/h each), total compute and parameters stay the same as one full-width head — diversity is free.

What are the shapes involved in MiniLM's multi-head attention (12 heads, d=384)?

Per layer: W_Q, W_K, W_V each 384×384 (organized as 12 blocks of 384×32), plus W_O 384×384. Per head: Q,K,V are n×32; scores n×n; per-head output n×32. Concat: n×384, times W_O → n×384. For a 512-token input: 12 attention matrices of 512×512 per layer. Fluently producing these shapes is exactly what "knows transformers" sounds like in an interview.

What do attention heads actually learn? How much should we trust interpretations?

Reproducible findings: positional heads (previous/next token), syntactic heads (verb↔arguments, determiner↔noun), coreference heads, rare-token/punctuation anchors — visible with tools like BERTviz. Caveats to state: many heads are diffuse or uninterpretable, attention weights aren't full explanations of model behavior (information also flows through values and the FFN), and 30–50% of heads are often prunable. Right posture: heads demonstrably specialize; clean human labels cover only a subset.

What is W_O for? Couldn't you just concatenate the heads?

Concatenation alone leaves each head's output stuck in its own 1/h slice of dimensions — head 3's finding could only ever influence dims 64–95 downstream. W_O linearly mixes across all heads' outputs, letting evidence combine (e.g., "the syntax head found the subject AND the coreference head agrees") and letting the network weight heads' contributions. It's also where head redundancy gets blended away.

Explain MQA/GQA in terms of heads (the modern serving question).

Multi-Query Attention: all query heads share ONE key/value head — K/V cache shrinks by h×, hugely cutting inference memory (M11.2), with some quality cost. Grouped-Query Attention: middle ground — groups of query heads share K/V heads (e.g., 32 Q-heads, 8 KV-heads in Llama-3). Motivation is purely serving economics: the KV cache, not compute, bottlenecks long-context inference. Answering this well signals you understand attention as a production system, not just a diagram.
