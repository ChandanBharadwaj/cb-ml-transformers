Where to go deeper: the papers, in reading order

You can now read the primary literature — the actual papers, not summaries of them. Each is listed with the module that prepared you for it; read after that module and the paper will feel like a review, not a wall.

  • "Efficient Estimation of Word Representations" (word2vec, 2013) — after M2. The contrastive idea, at the source.
  • "Attention Is All You Need" (2017) — after M5. The transformer paper; you now know every equation in it.
  • "BERT" (2018) — after M6.2. Masked LM and the encoder era.
  • "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" (GPT-3, 2020) — after M6.3. Scale and in-context learning.
  • "Sentence-BERT" (2019) — after M9.2. The bi-encoder that made RAG possible.
  • "MiniLM" (2020) — after M9.4. The distillation behind your embedder.
  • "Training Compute-Optimal LLMs" (Chinchilla, 2022) — after M7.4. The scaling laws.
  • "LoRA" (2021) — after M8.5. Low-rank fine-tuning.
  • "GLiNER" (2023) — after M10.2. NER as similarity, your second loop-closer.
  • "DeepSeek-R1" (2025) — after M11.5. Reasoning via RL on verifiable rewards, in the open.
Your three-project portfolio

The capstones you built across this course are a portfolio. Polished and written up, they demonstrate exactly the "missing middle" most applied-AI candidates can't show:

  • A fine-tuned domain embedder (M9.5): all-MiniLM fine-tuned with MNR loss on your own pairs, with recall@5 before/after — proves you understand embeddings deeply enough to improve them, not just call them.
  • A from-scratch RAG + reranker with a real eval harness (M12.1–12.3): retrieval, two-stage reranking, and a golden-set evaluation splitting retrieval from generation — proves you can build and measure a production-shaped system.
  • A framework-free agent over that RAG (M12.4): a ReAct loop with two tools from the raw API — proves you understand agents down to the token, not just the SDK.

Write each up with the numbers and one diagram (you can draw them now). Three projects, each with a metric and a mechanism you can explain to first principles, is a stronger signal than a dozen tutorial clones.

If you remember one thing

That is the difference between someone who calls the tools and someone who understands the machine. You are now the second kind of engineer.
